If you have ever run an AI model that you did not train yourself, you have
almost certainly pulled it from Hugging Face. It is where open models live. Not
one company's models, everybody's: Meta's, Google's, Mistral's, Alibaba's,
thousands of fine tunes by people you have never heard of. For most of a decade it
has worked the way a public utility works, which is to say nobody thought very
hard about who owned it.

On 3 September, Nvidia announced it is buying it.

## The numbers

The price is $12,930,300,000, written out to the hundred thousand in Nvidia's own
[announcement](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-to-acquire-hugging-face/).
The deal is contingent on regulatory approval.

What that buys, in the figures Nvidia gives: more than 18 million developers,
researchers and creators, more than 3 million models, 500,000 datasets, 1 million
applications and more than 200,000 companies using the platform to discover,
evaluate, customise and deploy AI.

For scale, Hugging Face's last disclosed private round was a $235 million Series C
in 2023 led by Salesforce Ventures. TechCrunch
[reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/03/nvidia-confirms-it-will-buy-hugging-face-for-12-9-billion/)
that the company turned down a $500 million offer from Nvidia last year. A
rejected half billion became an accepted thirteen billion in about twelve months,
which tells you roughly how the market repriced the thing in between.

## The promises

Give Nvidia this much: the commitments in the post are unusually concrete. Jensen
Huang wrote them himself, and they are not the usual acquisition mush about
synergies. In order:

- Hugging Face will remain an open platform for the entire AI ecosystem.
- Developers will choose the models they want, the frameworks they want, the
  clouds and inference service providers they want and the computing platforms
  they want.
- "NVIDIA compute will not be required to build on or deploy through Hugging
  Face."
- The platform will keep supporting open source and open weight models from every
  model builder and keep supporting multi-cloud and multi-accelerator
  development and deployment.

Nvidia also points out, fairly, that it is already the largest contributor of
open models and data to the platform: more than 500 models and more than 250 open
datasets. This is not a company that discovered open weights the week it wrote a
cheque.

Clem Delangue, Hugging Face's chief executive, framed it as a resourcing problem.
His account is that the open platform needed more compute, more support and more
visibility to work at the scale it now has to work at, and that this is why the
company went to talk to Jensen in the first place.

## The part that is not a mechanism

Here is the thing to hold on to. Every commitment above is a promise made by an
owner. Not one of them is a structure that would survive the owner changing its
mind.

<Pull>
"NVIDIA compute will not be required to build on or deploy through Hugging Face."
That sentence is the entire deal. It is a promise, not a mechanism.
</Pull>

There is no independent foundation holding the registry. No trust, no golden
share, no charter that a future chief executive would have to go to court to
unwind. The guarantee that the world's model registry stays neutral toward
hardware is, as of September, a paragraph on a corporate blog signed by a man who
sells the hardware.

That is not an accusation of bad faith. Huang's stated reasoning is coherent and
he has been making the open weights argument in public for a while, including in a
recent open letter he coauthored. It is a point about where a dependency now
sits. Before September, if Nvidia had wanted to make life quietly harder for
people deploying on AMD or on Google's accelerators, it had no particular lever at
the registry layer. After September, it owns the registry layer. The promise is
that it will not pull the lever. The lever still exists.

## What to actually watch

Press releases are not evidence. Behaviour over years is. Three things are worth
checking against the promise as this closes and settles:

**Does model discovery stay hardware neutral?** The ranking, the search, the
trending list and the default deployment buttons are the places where a thumb
would be invisible on the scale. Nobody would announce a change there.

**Do rival vendors keep first class support?** AMD, Google, Apple, Qualcomm and
the inference providers all publish to Hugging Face today. Watch whether their
integrations stay as good as Nvidia's, or merely stay possible.

**Does it stay cheap to leave?** The strongest guarantee of neutrality is not a
promise, it is a working export path. Weights are files. The healthiest outcome
for the open ecosystem is that mirrors and alternative registries get more
investment, not less, precisely because the main one now has a corporate parent.

Open models were supposed to be the hedge against a small number of companies
owning AI. That hedge has been running, for years, through infrastructure owned by
nobody in particular. From September it runs through infrastructure owned by the
most valuable chipmaker on earth. The models are still open. The commons is now
somebody's property.
